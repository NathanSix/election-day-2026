How to read this dashboard

1

What’s here

Who controls Congress, every statewide race on Georgia’s ballot plus three amendments, and the Senate races CBS News picked as most likely to decide the majority. The updates feed logs race calls and official announcements.

2

The colors

Blue is Democrat, red is Republican and purple is independent. On the amendments, teal is Yes and gold is No. A green pill means AP has called the race, a blinking pink pill means votes are being counted, and gray means counting hasn’t started.

3

Reading a race

Each bar is a candidate’s share of the votes counted so far. The thin pink bar under it shows how much of the expected vote AP thinks is in. Leads can grow, shrink or flip until that is nearly full, because early, mail and Election Day ballots are counted at different times.

4

What “called” means

A race shows as called only after the Associated Press declares a winner. AP does that once it concludes the trailing candidate has no realistic way to catch up, which can happen before every vote is counted. Until then, a lead is only a lead.

5

Control of Congress

The top bar is each chamber’s makeup before today. The AP tally counts the seats each party holds as races are called, including seats not on the ballot this year. The thin line marks the halfway point, and Majority shows how many seats control takes. A 50-50 Senate goes to the vice president’s party.

6

How it updates

Claude, an AI assistant, gathers AP race calls and Georgia Secretary of State counts on a schedule: every two hours during the day, then every hour from 7:20 PM to 2:20 AM ET tonight. The Updated pill shows when that last happened. This page also checks for new data every minute while it’s open. Use Live sources for real-time counts. Results are unofficial until certified.
